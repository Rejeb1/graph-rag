Marie Curie was a physicist and chemist born in Warsaw, Poland. She moved to Paris to study at the University of Paris. There she met Pierre Curie, a French physicist, and the two married in 1895. Marie Curie discovered the elements polonium and radium. She won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1903, shared with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel, and later won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1911, making her the first person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences.
