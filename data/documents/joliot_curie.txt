Irene Joliot-Curie was the elder daughter of Marie Curie and Pierre Curie, born in Paris in 1897. She married Frederic Joliot, a French chemist and physicist, in 1926, and the couple began publishing their research jointly as Irene and Frederic Joliot-Curie. Working together at the Radium Institute in Paris, which Marie Curie had founded, they discovered artificial radioactivity in 1934 by bombarding aluminium with alpha particles. For this discovery, Irene Joliot-Curie and Frederic Joliot-Curie were jointly awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1935, making the Curie family the recipients of a total of five Nobel Prizes across three laureates.
